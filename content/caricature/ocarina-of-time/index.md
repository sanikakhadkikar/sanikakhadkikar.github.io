Today, November 5, the remake of *The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time* came out on the Nintendo Switch 2, and thanks to a very generous coworker who let me try it on his Switch 2, I got to play it on day one. So this one is for him, and for a game that is almost exactly 28 years old.

## Sword fights in 3D

The original came out on the Nintendo 64 in November 1998, and it holds a record that has never been broken. It is the [only game ever to get a Metacritic score of 99](https://www.metacritic.com/game/the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time/), making it the highest-rated video game of all time.

{{< photo src="n64.jpg" alt="A black Nintendo 64 console with a cartridge inserted and a gray three-pronged controller" caption="The Nintendo 64, where Ocarina of Time first came out in 1998." by="Evan-Amos" source="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:N64-Console-Set.jpg" license="public domain" >}}

A lot of that comes from how much it invented. It was one of the first games to figure out how a sword fight should work in 3D, and its solution, called Z-targeting, lets you lock the camera onto an enemy and circle around it. That idea is now in [just about every action game](https://www.theringer.com/2018/10/23/video-games/legend-of-zelda-ocarina-time-best-game-ever-1998), along with its context-sensitive buttons that change what they do depending on where you are.

My favourite part is where it came from. The team was stuck on how Link could fight several enemies without all of them swarming him at once. Then, on a trip to Toei Kyoto Studio Park, a film studio theme park in Kyoto, they ducked into a theatre to escape the heat and watched a samurai show. A group of ninja surrounded the hero, but [only one attacked at a time](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/zelda-ocarina-of-time/1/2) while the others waited their turn. That is exactly how Z-targeting works. You lock onto one enemy, the rest hang back, and when you beat it you switch to the next. The director, Toru Osawa, even imagined an invisible kusarigama, the chain weapon one of the ninja was swinging, connecting Link to whatever he was targeting.

## One game, three timelines

The story is the other thing that made it stick. You play as Link, a boy trying to stop Ganondorf, the series' main villain, from taking over the kingdom of Hyrule. Princess Zelda gives Link a magic instrument, the Ocarina of Time, and playing it opens the door to a sword called the Master Sword. When Link pulls the sword out, he is too young to wield it, so he falls asleep and wakes up seven years later as an adult, in a world Ganondorf now rules. From then on, the sword and the ocarina let him travel back and forth between being a child and an adult. At the very end, Zelda uses the ocarina to send him back to his childhood.

That ending caused a 13-year argument. Every Zelda game is set in some version of Hyrule, and fans could never agree on how they fit together. If Link went back in time, what happened to the future he left behind? Nintendo finally answered in 2011, in a book called *Hyrule Historia*. Ocarina of Time [splits the series into three timelines](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Zelda_Timeline), one where Link returns to his childhood, one where the adult world he left carries on without him, and a third where Link loses to Ganondorf. Every Zelda game since sits on one of those three branches. So a single ending on a 1998 cartridge decides where the rest of the series happens.

## Why remake this one?

For the series' 40th anniversary, Nintendo could have remade anything, including the very first *Legend of Zelda* from 1986. Instead it picked Ocarina of Time, the first 3D Zelda and the game that defined what 3D Zelda would be. According to reporting on the remake, producer Eiji Aonuma chose it partly because [many of the current staff had limited familiarity with the older games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time_%282026_video_game%29), which I love. Remaking the classic is also a way of teaching it to a new team.

The remake keeps the original game but rebuilds almost everything around it, with [completely overhauled visuals, voiced cutscenes, expanded dialogue, orchestral music and reworked camera and controls](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/its-time-experience-the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-reborn-exclusively-for-nintendo-switch-2/). Critics seem to agree it worked. It currently has a Metacritic score of 96, making it the [best-reviewed game of 2026 so far](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/the-zelda-ocarina-of-time-remake-has-shot-to-the-top-of-metacritics-best-games-of-2026-so-far-makes-its-all-time-top-30/), although the 1998 original still holds the top spot.

## The little goose

An ocarina is a small, egg-shaped wind instrument, a kind of vessel flute where you blow into a hollow chamber instead of a tube. People have made instruments like this for thousands of years, including in pre-Columbian South America.

{{< photo src="ocarina-met.jpg" alt="A rounded clay vessel flute painted with red lines, with a mouthpiece and finger holes" caption="A ceramic ocarina from the Carchi culture of the northern Andes, now at the Metropolitan Museum of Art." credit="The Met, via Wikimedia Commons, CC0" credit_url="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ceramic_Ocarina_MET_1980.34.37.jpg" >}}

The modern version, and the name, come from Italy. In 1853, a 17-year-old in the town of Budrio near Bologna named Giuseppe Donati is said to have made his first one, and he called it *ocarina*, from the local Bolognese word for ["little goose"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocarina). Look at one from the side and you can see why.

Americans had their own name for it, the "sweet potato", also because of its shape. During the Second World War, cheap plastic sweet potatoes were something soldiers could carry anywhere, and TIME reported in 1943 that [sales of plastic ocarinas had skyrocketed](https://time.com/archive/6789254/music-from-mud-to-melody) since Pearl Harbor.

The ocarina in the game is much simpler than a real one. It plays only [five notes, D, F, A, B and the D above](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time/Ocarina_Songs), one on the A button and one on each of the four yellow C buttons of the N64 controller, and all twelve songs Link learns are built from just those five. Somehow that was enough. Before 1998, few people outside Italy and East Asia had ever held one, and afterwards ocarina makers like Songbird saw [demand grow dramatically](https://www.wfmt.com/2023/11/22/legend-of-zelda-and-the-rebirth-of-the-ocarina/), mostly from players who wanted to play Zelda's Lullaby for real.

## The remake on a Mac

Emulators, programs that pretend to be a console, have been around for decades. What surprised me is that the newest Switch 2 emulators run on Apple Silicon, the ARM chips inside MacBooks, [straight out of the box](https://tech-insider.org/switch-2-emulation-mario-kart-world-dk-bananza-pc-2026/). The Ocarina of Time remake was [booting on one of them, Lichii](https://thepcenthusiast.com/zelda-ocarina-of-time-remake-pc-emulator), within hours of its release. It still crashes and stutters, but a brand new console game running on a MacBook on day one is wild.

Emulators themselves are not illegal, but playing a game on one still means using your own copy of the game and your own console's encryption keys, and downloading either from the internet is piracy. Nintendo also takes this very seriously. In 2024 the team behind Yuzu, the most popular Switch emulator at the time, [settled with Nintendo for $2.4 million](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/console-gaming/developers-of-switch-emulator-yuzu-settle-with-nintendo-in-court-for-dollar24-million-less-than-a-week-after-being-sued) and shut down, and the projects that came after it keep getting takedown notices.

Sometimes I wish I had an ocarina of time of my own, so I could go back and learn the things I was not capable of in the past, but with everything I have experienced since.

Happy release day to Ocarina of Time, and thank you to my coworker for sharing his Switch 2. I owe you one.
