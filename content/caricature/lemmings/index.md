I randomly came across Norway lemmings this week, and until then I had no idea what they even were. It turns out a Norway lemming is a small, chubby rodent that lives in the mountains and tundra of Scandinavia, and it comes with one of the most famous animal myths in the world, that lemmings follow each other off cliffs. We even call people who blindly follow the crowd "***lemmings***". Except lemmings don't actually do this. And the myth has a surprisingly long reach, from a fake Disney documentary to Steve Jobs and the studio that makes GTA.

## Lemming years

{{< photo float="right" src="lemming-boat.jpg" alt="A Norway lemming with orange, black and cream fur crouching in the bottom of a blue boat next to a red wooden board" caption="A Norway lemming that fell into a boat in the Norwegian mountains." by="Arnstein Rønning" source="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lemmus_lemmus_no.JPG" license="CC BY-SA 3.0" >}}

Most years, you would barely notice them. Then, every three or four years, their numbers explode, and suddenly there are lemmings everywhere. Norwegians even have a word for it, *lemenår*, from *lemen* (lemming) and *år* (year). In the boom of 2011, a mountain warden in south-east Norway told NRK, Norway's public broadcaster, that he had [not seen so many since the late 1970s](https://www.newsinenglish.no/2011/05/05/predicted-lemming-year-arrives/), and up in Finnmark there were so many that dogs were catching and swallowing them.

## Falling from the sky

For centuries, nobody could explain where all of them suddenly came from. So people made something up. In the 1530s, a geographer named Zeigler of Strasbourg suggested that lemmings [fell out of the sky](https://www.mcgill.ca/oss/node/2587) during storms, and in 1555 the Swedish writer Olaus Magnus published a woodcut, a print made by carving a picture into a block of wood, inking it and stamping it onto paper, showing them raining down from the clouds. Not everyone was convinced. The Danish naturalist Ole Worm argued they were probably just blown in by the wind.

The real explanation is the cycle itself. Lemmings breed absurdly fast. A female can have her first litter when she is only a few weeks old, a pregnancy lasts around three weeks, and each litter has about [five to thirteen babies](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lemmus_lemmus). They can even keep breeding through winter in nests under the snow. So in a good year, their numbers jump roughly [every four years](https://www.sciencenews.org/blog/wild-things/were-only-noticing-snowy-owls), and the predators that eat them, like snowy owls, boom right after. When there are too many lemmings, they spread out looking for food and space, sometimes crossing rivers and lakes. Some of them drown. Nobody jumps on purpose.

## The cliff that Disney built

So where did the cliffs come from? Mostly from Walt Disney.

In 1958, Disney released *White Wilderness*, a nature documentary about the Arctic that went on to win the Academy Award for Best Documentary. Its most famous scene shows lemmings marching to the edge of a cliff and leaping into the sea. None of it was real. It was filmed in Alberta, a landlocked Canadian province with no sea and no native lemmings. The crew [bought lemmings from Manitoba](https://gizmodo.com/lemmings-dont-commit-mass-suicide-disney-pushed-them-o-1614038696), ran them on a turntable to make a small group look like a huge migration, and then pushed them off a cliff into the Bow River.

The truth came out in 1982, when the Canadian show *The Fifth Estate* investigated it in an episode called "Cruel Camera". By then, millions of people had grown up believing lemmings were suicidal. Honestly, this is disgusting and inhuman. A crew threw live animals off a cliff to fake a story about them, and the film won an Oscar. As far as I could find, Disney never faced any real consequences for it.

## The ad that walked off a cliff

The myth was so well known by the 1980s that Apple built an entire advert around it.

In 1984, Apple had made history with its Super Bowl advert for the Macintosh, which cast IBM as Big Brother. For the Super Bowl in January 1985, it tried to do it again with ["Lemmings"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemmings_(advertisement)), an ad for the Macintosh Office, a set of networked Macs, printers and a file server for businesses. It showed a line of blindfolded businesspeople whistling as they marched off a cliff, one after another, The businesspeople stood for everyone who kept buying IBM computers just because everyone else did, and the message was that only those who switched to Apple would avoid the drop.

It was a disaster. Calling your potential customers lemmings was not a great sales pitch, and the stadium reportedly watched it in stunned silence. The product was not even ready. The file server it promised did not ship until 1987. Steve Jobs had pushed hard for the campaign, and the flop added to the pressure on him. Three months later, the CEO John Sculley asked Apple's board to remove Jobs from running the Macintosh division, and by the end of the year Jobs had left the company he co-founded.

## From Lemmings to GTA

The lemming myth also made a video game company. In 1991, a small studio in Dundee, Scotland, called DMA Design released [*Lemmings*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemmings_(video_game)), a puzzle game where you guide a stream of tiny, mindless lemmings across a level. Left alone, they walk straight off ledges and into traps, so you have to assign some of them jobs, like digging, building bridges or blocking the others, to get as many as possible safely to the exit. It became one of the best-selling games of its time, and Dundee is still proud of it.

{{< photo float="right" src="dundee-statue.jpg" alt="Three small bronze lemmings with big hair climbing up a stone pillar in a park, one at the top, one halfway and one at the bottom holding a sign" caption="Bronze lemmings climbing a pillar on Perth Road in Dundee, a tribute to the game." credit="Platygyra, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons" credit_url="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lemmings_statue.jpg" >}}

The success of *Lemmings* kept DMA Design going, and in 1997 the same studio released a top-down crime game called *Grand Theft Auto*. A few years later, DMA Design became [Rockstar North](https://kotaku.com/what-do-grand-theft-auto-and-lemmings-have-in-common-5830192), part of Rockstar Games, the company we all know for *GTA* and *Red Dead Redemption*. So the next time someone bets on whether [Jesus will return before GTA VI](/caricature/prediction-markets/), remember it all started with tiny rodents walking off a cliff.

Lemmings never jumped off anything. But we sure are good at jumping to conclusions, and sometimes at forcing them.
